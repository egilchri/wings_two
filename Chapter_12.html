Chapter 12

Chapter Summary & Character Study
Summary

On a Thursday, Densher and Kate meet at her Aunt Maud's house. Kate explains that they cannot use the American ladies, Milly and Mrs. Stringham, to help them meet secretly, because the ladies would feel obligated to tell Aunt Maud. Densher expresses frustration, saying this proves their situation is unworkable.

Kate disagrees, telling him that Milly likes him and that he should "make something of that." They are meeting in a private room, which Kate says Aunt Maud has allowed for this one time. Feeling constrained, Densher asks Kate, "Will you take me just as I am?" She asks him to trust her and give her time. He then grabs her, asks if she loves him, and they embrace.

After the embrace, they continue to discuss their situation. Kate explains that Aunt Maud is not approving, just indifferent to details, and that they cannot meet alone in the house again. She proposes a new plan: she claims that Milly is in love with Densher. Kate says that since Milly knows about Aunt Maud's opposition to them, Milly will want to see Densher to "console" him. Densher is confused, assuming Milly knows that he and Kate are in love. Kate reveals that she has never told Milly about their relationship and that Aunt Maud’s strategy is to ignore it, so she would not have mentioned it either. According to Kate, Milly only sees that Densher likes Kate and that Kate is good to him in return.

Densher asks Kate to come to his rooms instead, but she avoids answering. He asks if her plan involves making Milly think that she (Kate) hates him, which Kate dismisses. As she sees him out, she repeats her instruction that he must go see Milly.

Character Study

Based on the text, here is a concise character study:

  • *Densher** is presented as frustrated and impatient with his and Kate’s secretive situation, which he feels is untenable ("a nail...in the coffin of our odious delay"). He seeks directness and a clear resolution, demanding, "Will you take me just as I am?" His initial state is one of agitation and a sense of being trapped in "bondage." However, his situation shifts dramatically when confronted with Kate's affection. An emotional outburst where he demands to know if she loves him is immediately quelled by her physical "surrender." This act pacifies him completely, shifting him from a state of confrontation to one of confused but generous compliance, agreeing to "leave everything to her" despite not fully understanding her complex plans.
  • *Kate Croy** is presented as controlled, strategic, and self-assured. Described as "bright and handsome," she meets Densher’s frustration with high spirits and a prepared plan. She skillfully evades his ultimatums ("dodging the ultimatum") and uses emotional and physical intimacy as a tool to manage his impatience and secure his trust. Her situation does not shift; rather, she solidifies her control. She systematically reveals her plan to use Milly Theale as an unwitting means for them to meet, demonstrating a pragmatic and manipulative intelligence. She carefully manages information, revealing only at the end that Milly is completely unaware of their relationship, a fact that re-frames the entire dynamic for Densher.
  • *Aunt Maud** and **Milly Theale** are presented entirely through Kate and Densher's conversation. Aunt Maud is characterized as a powerful but distant obstacle who operates with an "absence of smallness," allowing small liberties (like this meeting) while holding firm on the larger issue. Milly is presented by Kate as a kind, "exquisite," and naive pawn who is fond of Densher and can therefore be used to "help" their cause without understanding her role in it.
