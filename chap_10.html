Densher realized, all over again, that he didn't like his hotel. It bothered him even more because he'd disliked it before. The place was packed with tourists from all over the world at this time of year—mostly German, American, and English—and it really grated on him. It was loud and unpleasant, anything but Italian, anything but Venetian. He knew Venetian was just a dialect, but it was like pure poetry compared to some of the other languages spoken in that noisy hotel.

He often found it both enjoyable and irritating to realize that he'd already experienced everything before, during his travels. He'd visited Venice three or four times already, and each time he'd gotten annoyed by the hotel and wanted to escape the cacophony of the common areas, the friendly American families, and the overfed German staff.
