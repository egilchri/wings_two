Chapter 20

Chapter Summary & Character Study
Summary

A woman, Milly, is alone in her rented Venetian palace. She reflects on how her man of affairs, Eugenio, found the palace for her and recently escorted her from Paris. She also recalls that her recent travels have been crowded with friends and acquaintances.

Milly appreciates her solitude, which Eugenio arranged by taking her companions away for the morning at her unspoken request. She walks through the entire apartment, deciding she wants to stay there permanently and resolving to ask Eugenio to arrange it.

When she returns to the main saloon, she finds Lord Mark waiting for her. He says he came to Venice on an impulse after hearing where she was from their mutual friends. He expresses no interest in joining the others who are out. Milly offers to show him around the palace, and he accepts.

Character Study

Based on the text, here is a concise character study:

  • *Milly:**
    • Milly is presented as a wealthy but frail young woman who, after weeks of being surrounded by people, is experiencing solitude for the first time in her grand new residence. This solitude allows her to feel a sense of ownership and peace. She is acutely aware of her condition, a fact she guards with a "systematic bravado" and a "principle of pride." She views her relationships as complex performances, especially with Kate. Her situation shifts abruptly when her cherished solitude is interrupted by the arrival of Lord Mark, which transforms her mood from reflective relief to a guarded suspicion of his motives.
  • *Eugenio:**
    • Presented as a supremely competent, theatrical, and expensive servant, Eugenio is initially judged by Milly to be a "swindler finished to the finger-tips." This mutual, unspoken understanding of his mercenary nature has paradoxically become the basis of their trust and "happy relation." He intuits Milly's needs perfectly, having arranged her accommodations and now managing her companions to give her time alone. His role shifts in Milly's mind from a facilitator to a potential agent who might use her wealth to practically counter her fate.
  • *Kate Croy:**
    • Kate is portrayed as a cool, intelligent, and "mutually watchful" companion to Milly. Their friendship is defined by a shared pretense; their private moments are staged "puttings-off of the mask" that conceal more than they reveal. She understands Milly’s condition and the need for "duplicity" to avoid upsetting her fragile state. She is described as a "lady of her court" circling the central, still figure of Milly.
  • *Lord Mark:**
    • Lord Mark appears as a patient and charming man who arrives unannounced. Initially, Milly is glad to see him, recalling a past kindness. However, his situation immediately shifts in her perception from a welcome acquaintance to a man with a singular, unnerving "intention" when he shows a pointed lack of interest in seeing anyone but her.
  • *Other Characters:**
  • **Susie (Mrs. Stringham):** A loyal companion whom Milly pities for having to be "so sorry and to say so little about it." She is primarily concerned with Milly’s immediate well-being.
  • **Mrs. Lowder (Aunt Maud):** A social orchestrator, part of the overwhelming crowd that Milly has been trying to escape.
  • **Sir Luke Strett:** A doctor, mentioned in contrast to Eugenio, whose approach to Milly's problem is presented as less practical and more abstract than Eugenio's monetary one.
