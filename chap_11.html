Chapter II

Chapter Summary & Character Study
Summary

Merton Densher arrives by train at Euston station at five o'clock. Kate, whom he had wired from Liverpool, meets him there. They go to the station's refreshment room for tea and talk for about half an hour. Kate says she must be at Lancaster Gate by six. Before parting, she suggests they meet the next morning at the National Gallery.

The following day, they meet at the gallery. They sit and talk, but Kate gets up abruptly. Densher remarks on the closeness of the room, and they move to another part of the building. There, they encounter a young American woman, Miss Theale, whom Densher knows from New York. Kate and Densher have lunch with Miss Theale and her companion.

After lunch, Densher and Kate leave the two women. Soon after, Kate asks Densher to get her a cab, and they separate. Densher then takes a long walk, ending up in Regent's Park, where he sits on a bench. Before they parted, Densher had asked Kate if her Aunt Maud knew of his return. Kate said she did not, but that she would tell her that night and arrange for him to visit. They made an appointment to meet on Thursday at Lancaster Gate. After sitting for a while, Densher gets up and resumes his walk.

Character Study

Based on the text, here is a concise character study:

  • *Merton Densher:**
    • Densher is presented as a man returning to a situation he finds both intensely desirable and maddeningly frustrating. His immediate state is one of heightened impatience and "personal need" for Kate, fueled by their separation. He arrives with a clear, romantic goal: to end their clandestine waiting. However, this resolve is immediately confronted by logistical and social barriers, shifting his mood from passion to analytical suspicion. He feels managed by Kate and grows apprehensive, suspecting she has "preparations...in hand" that he doesn't understand. His focus shifts from the simple joy of reunion to trying to decipher Kate’s strategy and the new complications in their affair.
  • *Kate:**
    • Kate is portrayed, through Densher’s perspective, as pragmatic, proactive, and socially adept. Upon their reunion, she immediately takes control of their arrangements, navigating public spaces and social obligations (Aunt Maud) with an "expertness" that both impresses and frustrates Densher. She is presented as a master of managing their difficult situation, using charm and decisive action to deflect confrontation and direct their encounters. Her situation has shifted from simply waiting for Densher to actively orchestrating a more complex scenario involving him, her aunt, and a new acquaintance. She assures him her "cleverness...has grown infernal."
  • *Miss Theale (Milly):**
Miss Theale is introduced not as a developed character, but as a new and significant factor in Kate and Densher's situation. Her sudden prominence is an immediate shift in their dynamic. Densher perceives her as unexpectedly occupying the "foreground" of his relationship with Kate. Her presence transforms their affair from a private struggle into a more complicated social arrangement, positioning her as either an obstacle or, as Densher speculates, a "convenience" in Kate's larger plan.
