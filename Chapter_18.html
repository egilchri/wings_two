Chapter 18

Chapter Summary & Character Study
Summary

After Kate and Densher leave, Milly and Mrs. Stringham (Susie) are alone. Milly asks Susie about Kate, and Susie says Kate "knew" about her meeting with the doctor, Sir Luke Strett. Milly tells Susie that she and the doctor must "see me through." They embrace, and Milly insists she doesn't want to know the details of what the doctor said, only that Susie will help her follow his instructions. Susie reports that the doctor told her she can help Milly and that Milly must do as she likes. Milly asks Susie for her opinion of Mr. Densher, and Susie calls him handsome. Milly says the doctor's advice to her is "to live."

The next morning, Susie visits Mrs. Lowder and cries. She then recounts her meeting with Sir Luke. Susie explains that the doctor confirmed Milly does not have the illness she feared, but he found something else. The doctor did not tell her what it is, and instructed that Milly must be made happy. Milly is to return to see him in three months.

Mrs. Lowder and Susie discuss this, interpreting it to mean Milly should marry Merton Densher. Susie asks for Mrs. Lowder's help. Mrs. Lowder reveals that her niece Kate thinks she cares for Densher but is mistaken. She instructs Susie to deny this if asked. Mrs. Lowder then proposes a plan: Milly and Susie will stay, and she will host a dinner for them to meet Densher. Mrs. Lowder says she is giving up her own higher ambitions for Milly's marriage. She and Susie agree that Densher is charming.

Character Study

Based on the text, here is a concise character study:

  • *Milly**
  • **Presentation:** Milly is presented as a determined and proactive "fighter" who, upon sensing bad news, immediately takes control. She exhibits a sudden burst of energetic, almost performative brightness, reversing the dynamic with her companion by offering comfort instead of receiving it. She is insistent on not knowing the details of her condition, framing her future as a collaborative project for others to manage under her direction ("you must simply see me through").
  • **Situation Shift:** Having received what is clearly a grave, though unspecified, diagnosis via her companion, Milly’s situation shifts from uncertainty to crisis. Her immediate reaction is to actively manage the emotional fallout of those around her, establishing herself as the strong, directing figure.
  • *Mrs. Stringham (Susie)**
  • **Presentation:** Initially, she is overcome with pity and a "torment of helplessness." She is emotionally transparent, struggling to conceal her distress from Milly. She is presented as deeply loyal and caring but passive, requiring explicit direction first from the doctor, then from Milly, and finally from Mrs. Lowder.
  • **Situation Shift:** Her role transforms from a simple companion into the keeper of a terrible secret and a reluctant agent in a plan to ensure Milly's "happiness." She is moved from a state of passive support to one of active, emotionally draining crisis management.
  • *Mrs. Lowder (Maud Manningham)**
  • **Presentation:** Depicted as a pragmatic, commanding, and unsentimental strategist. She is emotionally contained, treating her friend's tears as an administrative issue to be waited out. She immediately assesses the situation in terms of leverage and opportunity, formulating a plan of action. She is candid about her willingness to manipulate and "lie well" to achieve her goals.
  • **Situation Shift:** Mrs. Lowder is drawn into the central drama as a confidante. Her role immediately evolves into that of a chief strategist, manipulating the situation to serve her own interests regarding her niece, Kate, while ostensibly helping Milly.
  • *Kate Croy and Merton Densher (as presented by others)**
  • **Presentation:** These characters are presented entirely through the perceptions of others. Kate is seen as perceptive, "charming," and self-sufficient. Mrs. Lowder also portrays her as deluded in her affections for Densher. Densher is described primarily by his surface qualities as "handsome" and "charming," and is viewed as an instrument or a "remedy" in the scheme to make Milly happy.
  • **Situation Shift:** Unknowingly, their status shifts from being Milly's guests to becoming central, albeit passive, figures in a newly formed plot concerning Milly’s well-being and Kate's romantic future.
