Chapter Summary & Character Study
Summary

Milly and Lord Mark are in her apartment overlooking a canal. She tells him she never wants to go down, that she no longer goes out, and that her adventure is staying where she is. He asks if she is unwell. In response, she kneels at the window and silently drops her face into her arms.

When she speaks again, she tells him she would like to die there. He laughs, then suggests she buy the house. She says she will try. He reminds her of a previous offer he made to look after her. After more conversation, she tells him directly that she is very badly ill. He asks if she is getting advice, and she says she is.

Lord Mark tells Milly that she needs to be adored and proposes to do it himself, asking her to believe in him. Milly laughs at his delivery. She turns him down, saying she cannot make a bargain. She then tells him he shouldn't have come to see her, but rather his older friends, suggesting he should pursue Kate Croy, which is what Mrs. Lowder wants.

Lord Mark asks if Kate is available, suggesting she is in love with another man. Milly denies this, saying that Kate herself gave her word she is free. Lord Mark questions the value of Kate’s word.

As he prepares to leave, a servant brings a visiting card. Milly is told the gentleman is waiting upstairs. She agrees to see him and announces to Lord Mark that the visitor is Merton Densher. Lord Mark says, "Oh!"

Character Study

Based on the text, here is a concise character study:

  • *Milly**
  • **Initial Presentation:** Milly is presented as physically isolated and emotionally melancholic. She romanticizes her confinement in a Venetian palace, viewing it as a fortress and a "divine dustless air" from which she never wants to descend.
  • **Character Revelation:** Though physically fragile—collapsing to her knees when directly asked about her health—she possesses a sharp, analytical mind. She dissects Lord Mark's potential motives with cynical precision, even theorizing her illness could be a financial attraction. She is guarded but becomes starkly direct when pressed, stating plainly, "I'm very badly ill." She is assertive in her rejection of his advances and strategically redirects the conversation to his relationship with Kate Croy.
  • **Situational Shift:** The scene forces Milly from a state of wistful fantasy into a direct verbal confrontation with her mortality. Her composure, regained after her confession, is shattered by the unexpected arrival of Merton Densher, which is described as a "blow to her presence of mind."
  • *Lord Mark**
  • **Initial Presentation:** He is a polite, socially adept visitor who initially responds to Milly's mood with light, aesthetic commentary ("Veronese costumes"), failing to grasp its deeper meaning. He is described as "adequately human" but operating within the bounds of social convention.
  • **Character Revelation:** He is perceptive enough to notice Milly's confinement and ask about her health, but he is fundamentally out of his depth when faced with the reality of her illness. Milly perceives him as a man of "pleasant commerce" who is unnerved by the "sinister light of tragedy." His marriage proposal lacks emotional force and he appears awkward and ineffective when confronted with a reality he cannot manage. He is also shown to be persistent and skeptical, particularly regarding Kate Croy's affections.
  • **Situational Shift:** He arrives as a confident suitor but is systematically disarmed. First, he is confronted with Milly's severe illness; second, his proposal is gently but firmly rejected; and third, he is confronted by the arrival of a rival, whose name prompts a loaded reaction. He ends the scene diminished and outmaneuvered.
  • *Merton Densher**
  • **Presentation:** Densher is not physically present until the final moments and does not speak. He is characterized entirely by the dramatic effect his arrival has on the other characters.
  • **Situational Shift:** His sudden appearance serves as the scene's climax, shattering the tense equilibrium. His arrival is a shock to Milly and provokes a significant, judgmental reaction from Lord Mark, instantly establishing him as a figure of central importance to their conflict.
